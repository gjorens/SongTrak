Getting started with SongTrak




1. View a song


    

  • Tap a song in the list to see its chords above the lyrics.
    • 

  • The key and capo controls (the Key tab in Play mode, and the Edit screen) are explained in section 3.
    • 

  • When a setlist is open, the skip buttons either side of Play move to the previous or next song.
    • 



What the buttons mean


    

  • + adds a song.
    • 

  • The tray with an arrow imports.
    • 

  • The right-pointing triangle plays.
    • 

  • The box with an up arrow shares.
    • 

  • The pencil edits.
    • 

  • Press and hold a button, or point at it on a computer, to see its name.
    • 



More info…
    

  • The chart shows the section names, the chords above the words and the lyrics.
    • 

  • Text size and colours are in Settings (the ⚙ button).
    • 

  • To play along with auto-scroll, tap Play.
    • 



2. Add a song


Adding


    

  • Tap the + button (Add song), type or paste a chord sheet, and fill in the title and artist.
    • 

  • Chords go in square brackets before the word, like [G]Hello.
    • 

  • Section names start with #, like # Verse.
    • 

  • Set the key as described in section 3, then add Style, Mood and Keywords. Keywords you have used before appear in a drop-down.
    • 



The editor screen


    

  • Save song, Cancel and Delete song are at the top, beside the search button. The Setlists box is at the bottom.
    • 

  • On a narrower screen, such as the iPad held upright, the editor fills the whole screen while you edit.
    • 



Edit tools


    

  • Chords and Sections buttons insert them into the song text.
    • 

  • The four Cursor arrows move the cursor in the song text (touch and hold to repeat). Hold the Shift key on a keyboard while you tap an arrow, or turn on Select, to select text as the cursor moves.
    • 

  • Copy, Cut and Paste work on the selected text.
    • 

  • Expand repeated sections fills any empty section heading, or a line such as (Chorus), with a copy of the section of that name.
    • 



Undo and Redo


    

  • Undo and Redo (in the Cursor row) step back and forward through changes to the song text.
    • 

  • One step is a burst of typing, or one tap of Chord, Section, Paste, Cut, Pause or Expand repeated sections.
    • 

  • They cover the song text only, and the history is cleared when you close the editor.
    • 



More info…
    

  • A song is plain text in a ChordPro-style format: a few {key: value} lines at the top (title, artist, key and so on), then the lyrics with chords in square brackets and section names starting with #.
    • 

  • If you paste a chart from a chord website, where the chords sit on their own line above the words, or sections are marked like [Verse 1], SongTrak converts it for you.
    • 

  • Those websites usually do not copy the song name and artist, so type them in.
    • 



3. Key, capo and chords


Finding the key


    

  • While adding or editing a song, tap Detect key next to Original Key.
    • 

  • SongTrak looks at all the chords and asks which of the 24 possible keys (12 major and 12 minor) fits them best.
    • 

  • A key fits when the song uses the chords that belong to it (for example G, Am, Bm, C, D, Em and F♯dim in G major) and starts and ends on that key’s home chord.
    • 

  • It shows its best guess and two other possibilities, and says “close call” when it is not sure.
    • 

  • A song that changes key partway can fool it, so always check the result.
    • 



Changing the key


    

  • Original Key is the key of the recording. It is never changed by My Key.
    • 

  • My Key is the key you want it to sound in. Pick it and the app works out the capo for you.
    • 

  • Or choose Rewrite the chords to rewrite the chords into My Key with no capo.
    • 

  • Above a song you can also use the Key control to transpose (the number of half-tones is shown) and the capo − / + buttons.
    • 

  • When you change either, a bar appears so you can Save or Discard.
    • 



How it is stored


    

  • Each song keeps four separate things: its Original Key, your My Key, the capo, and whether the chords were rewritten.
    • 

  • Save keeps them with that song. Discard goes back to the last saved ones.
    • 

  • They are part of every backup.
    • 



Export


    

  • With “Include song data” on (and when you use Share), the file has the lines {key}, {capo}, {mkey} and {mkr}: the Original Key, capo, My Key and rewritten setting.
    • 

  • With it off, the file is plain ChordPro for other apps and has only {key} and {capo}. The key is the one the chords on the page are written in, so other apps show the right chords.
    • 



Import


    

  • SongTrak reads {key}, {capo}, {mkey} and {mkr} when they are there.
    • 

  • If the file has no key, it detects one from the chords.
    • 

  • If the song is already in your library you are asked whether to update it (its key, capo and My Key are then replaced by the file’s) or keep both.
    • 



More info…


How the guess is scored


    

  • Each chord in square brackets is read as a root note and a type: major, minor or diminished.
    • 

  • For each of the 24 keys, a chord that belongs to that key’s scale adds points, and a chord that does not takes away 60% as many.
    • 

  • The first chord counts three times and the last chord four times as much as the others, because songs usually start and end on the home chord.
    • 

  • There is an extra bonus when the first or last chord is exactly the key’s own chord (G in G major, Em in E minor).
    • 

  • In a minor key, the major chord a fifth above the home chord (B in E minor) also counts as belonging, because it is very common.
    • 

  • The highest score wins. If it beats the runner-up by less than 3 points, the result is called a close call.
    • 



Capo or rewrite


    

  • My Key is where the song sounds.
    • 

  • With a capo you play easier shapes and the capo raises them to My Key.
    • 

  • If you rewrite the chords instead, the chords shown are changed into My Key and no capo is needed. The chords saved with the song are then written in the key you play, which is My Key less any capo.
    • 

  • Either way, the Original Key is left alone.
    • 



In the files


    

  • The lines look like {key: A}, {capo: 6}, {mkey: C} and {mkr: 0}.
    • 

  • A capo number from 0 to 11 is accepted.
    • 

  • {mkr: 1} is only used when {mkey} is there too.
    • 





4. Find songs


    

  • Search by title, artist or keyword.
    • 

  • Sort by title, artist, date added and more. A leading "The" is ignored when sorting.
    • 

  • The Songs ▾ menu switches between All Songs and any setlist.
    • 

  • On a wide screen, drag the thin bar on the right edge of the left panel to make it wider or narrower. Double tap it to go back to the normal width. SongTrak remembers the width on this device.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Search looks at the title, artist and keywords.
    • 

  • The Style, Mood and Keyword menus narrow the list, and a banner with a Clear button shows while a filter is on.
    • 

  • The A to Z strip at the side jumps to songs that start with that letter.
    • 



5. Setlists


Making and editing


    

  • Add a song to a setlist from the drop-down in the song view.
    • 

  • Open Setlists to create, rename, delete and reorder them.
    • 

  • Manage hides the left panel to give the three columns the full width. Tap the large orange ‹ Done button in the top left corner to come back.
    • 



In the middle column


    

  • ▲ and ▼ move a song up or down one place with a short slide.
    • 

  • Swipe a song left to show a red Remove button that takes it out of the setlist (the black Undo bar puts it back).
    • 

  • Tap a song to highlight it (double-tap opens it).
    • 



Playing a setlist


    

  • When a setlist is showing, the sort list has Set order.
    • 

  • In Set order, ▲ and ▼ beside each song move it up or down one place; the song slides to its new spot and flashes.
    • 

  • Use the previous and next song buttons to step through the set.
    • 



Deleting a setlist


    

  • Delete removes only the setlist and keeps all its songs.
    • 

  • To delete the songs too, tick Also delete its songs from my library. The box then lists any other setlists that would lose those songs.
    • 

  • The dialog starts on Cancel.
    • 



More info…
    

  • A setlist is a named list of songs in an order you choose, and one song can be in several setlists.
    • 

  • In the Manage view, drag the thin bar between two columns to change their widths (double-click or double-tap a bar to reset). SongTrak remembers the widths on this device.
    • 

  • The right-hand column has an A–Z strip: touch or drag along it to jump to songs starting with that letter.
    • 

  • Setlists are saved in your library, so they are in your backups, and a shared setlist file carries all its songs.
    • 



6. Play and drums


The Play screen


    

  • Use Play mode on stage for a larger, uncluttered view.
    • 

  • The song title scrolls up out of the way as you play. Syncing never interrupts a song while you are playing.
    • 

  • The text size buttons are at the left of the bottom bar, beside the previous and next song buttons.
    • 

  • The bottom bar keeps only the action buttons: start and stop the drums and loops, scroll, next and previous song.
    • 

  • The scroll Speed goes from 1 (slowest) to 15, and each song remembers its own.
    • 

  • Tick Scroll starts with the drums and the scroll begins when the count-in ends. Scrolling can then be paused, restarted or sped up without affecting the drums or loops.
    • 



The tabs and drawers


    

  • Coloured tabs on the right edge slide out drawers: Drums, Loops, Mixer, Pedals, Tuner and Key.
    • 

  • Tap a tab again to close its drawer, or tap anywhere outside an open drawer.
    • 

  • In ⚙ Settings, under Play mode, you can untick tabs you do not use, drag them into any order, and choose which tab opens by itself when Play starts (for example Key). SongTrak remembers this on this device.
    • 



Tabs fold away on a phone


    

  • On a phone, or a tablet held upright, the tabs slide away over about two seconds, leaving a small arrow handle on the right edge. Tap the handle to bring them back.
    • 

  • They start to go: 2 seconds after you close a drawer; 5 seconds after Play opens if you open no drawer; or 10 seconds after you bring them back with the arrow if you pick no tab.
    • 

  • Settings > Play mode lets you change the 2 seconds to 5 seconds, or turn the folding off.
    • 



The Key tab


    

  • When you change the key or capo, the Key tab shows a yellow dot. The Save and Discard box stays in place at the top of the drawer (grey until you change something).
    • 

  • Original key: fix it when the song's key is wrong. The chords are not changed.
    • 

  • Preferred chording key: transposes the chords.
    • 

  • Capo position and Resulting key (the sounding key): changing one moves the other, so they always follow each other.
    • 



Drums: pattern and feel


    

  • Open the Drums tab for a backing beat.
    • 

  • Choose a pattern (rock, funk, ride, ska, disco, tambourine, clap, cowbell, toms and more).
    • 

  • Set the tempo (or tap it), and add swing or a human feel.
    • 

  • Each song remembers its own drum settings and shares them with the song file.
    • 



Starting and ending the drums


    

  • Stop ends the drums at once.
    • 

  • Fade ends them the way the Fade drop-down (next to Count-in, kept with the song) says: fade out over 5 or 10 seconds or over 1, 2 or 4 bars.
    • 

  • Finish bar + flourish: the bar finishes, then a crash and kick ring out.
    • 

  • Fill + flourish: the bar finishes, a fill bar plays, then the crash and kick.
    • 

  • Any loops fade away too.
    • 



Edit the beat


    

  • The Edit the beat grid is always open under the pattern controls.
    • 

  • Tap a square once for a hit, again for a soft hit, and again for a rest.
    • 

  • The sound names stay in place on the left while you scroll the grid sideways.
    • 

  • Tap More sounds for crash, ride, toms, clap, cowbell, shaker and tambourine.
    • 

  • Save as my pattern keeps a beat under a name in My patterns in the Pattern list.
    • 



Sound levels


    

  • Sound levels (above the grid) has a slider for each drum sound. Turn up any you cannot hear (let go of a slider to hear it) and turn down others.
    • 

  • Small-speaker kick adds a higher thump so the kick is audible on a phone or iPad speaker.
    • 

  • Tick Save these levels with this song to give a song its own levels. Otherwise one set of levels is used for every song on this device.
    • 



Tap in


    

  • Tap in… opens a pop-up with four big pads (Kick, Snare, Hi-hat and Open hat to start with). The drop-down under each pad changes its sound.
    • 

  • Tap Start, listen to the count-in, then tap the pads in time while the beat loops round.
    • 

  • Each tap sounds at once, is placed on the nearest step, and is saved in the beat as you go.
    • 

  • Keep looping to add more drums. Use Undo or Clear these 4 to redo, and turn the click off if you do not want it.
    • 

  • Tap Exit when you are done, then fine-tune on the grid.
    • 



Beat length


    

  • The Beat length row sets 1 to 4 bars (new beats start at 4 bars), with a tab for each bar.
    • 



Turnaround and fills


    

  • The Turnaround row plays a fill bar after every 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 16 bars of the beat.
    • 

  • The fill is an extra bar, so every bar of your pattern still gets played. With a 4-bar beat and "After 2 bars" you hear bar 1, bar 2, Fill 1, bar 3, bar 4, Fill 2, and so on.
    • 

  • It rotates through four different one-bar fills (Fill 1 to 4). You change each on its tab.
    • 

  • A line under these rows tells you what you are looking at.
    • 

  • Your fills are kept even if you turn the Turnaround off.
    • 

  • Save fills as keeps the set of four under My fills for any song.
    • 



A 12-bar blues


    

  • Pick Blues shuffle, Slow blues or Texas shuffle.
    • 

  • Set Beat length to 1 bar and the Turnaround to After 11 bars.
    • 

  • You hear 11 bars of the groove and a fill as bar 12, which leads into the next time round.
    • 



More info…
    

  • The drum sounds are made by the app, not recordings.
    • 

  • Each song keeps its own beat: pattern, tempo, swing, human feel, count-in, bars, turnaround and fills. Changing one song never changes another.
    • 

  • Swing delays the off-beat notes.
    • 

  • Human feel adds small random changes in timing and strength.
    • 

  • Count-in plays clicks before the beat: None, 1 or 2 bars of clicks, a drum Fill only (the first of your fills), or 1 or 2 bars of clicks followed by that fill. The scroll waits until the count-in has finished.
    • 

  • My patterns and My fills are shared by all songs and are saved in your library and backups.
    • 

  • The Turnaround plays its fill after the chosen number of bars, counted from when the drums start, and again from the moment you change the beat while it plays.
    • 



7. Listen and find chords


    

  • ♫ Listen & find ▾ opens a search for the song in Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music or YouTube.
    • 

  • It can also search Ultimate Guitar, Chordie, E-Chords, Songsterr or Google to find chords and tabs.
    • 

  • Copy a chart you find, then use Import → From clipboard.
    • 



More info…
    

  • These links open a search in a new tab.
    • 

  • The song title and artist are also copied to the clipboard first. If a website sends you to its app (on an iPhone, Ultimate Guitar can send you to the App Store) and the app does not carry your search over, tap its search box and paste.
    • 

  • SongTrak does not connect to those websites and cannot see your accounts there.
    • 

  • Copy the chart you find, then use Import, then From clipboard.
    • 



Tuner and tunings


    

  • 🎸 Tune opens a guitar tuner. Tap Start listening, allow the microphone, and pluck one string.
    • 

  • The needle shows how far off you are and turns green when you are in tune.
    • 

  • Tap a string to hear its note.
    • 

  • Choose a tuning such as Drop D or DADGAD, or enter your own notes.
    • 

  • Edit a song to set its Tuning, and it shows next to the key. A song in a different tuning is marked in the song list.
    • 



More info…
    

  • The tuner listens through the microphone, or through the input you choose under Input. The line below it shows which input is really in use and how many your device lists.
    • 

  • It works out the note being played. Nothing you play is recorded or sent anywhere.
    • 

  • Chromatic shows any note instead of the nearest string.
    • 

  • A4 is 440 Hz unless you change it (400 to 480).
    • 

  • Use for this song saves the tuning with that song.
    • 



Loops and foot pedal


    

  • The Loops tab on the right of the Play screen opens a simple looper. Record one track, then add others on top. The first recording sets how long the loop is.
    • 

  • Tap Rec to record, Dub to add a layer, Undo to step back, and Fade out to finish.
    • 

  • With Start and stop with the drums and scroll ticked, Rec starts the drums and the scroll too, and plays any loops you already have while it records the new one.
    • 

  • A foot pedal can start and stop recording so your hands stay on the guitar.
    • 

  • You can hide the Loops tab with Loops tab under Play mode in ⚙ settings.
    • 



More info…


Tracks and inputs


    

  • There are three tracks: Guitar, Voice and Bass. You can choose the audio input for each.
    • 

  • Under each input is a channel choice: Input 1 (left), Input 2 (right) or Both mixed (mono). With a two-input audio interface such as a Scarlett, choose the channel the guitar or microphone is plugged into.
    • 

  • The default, Default, uses the device as it is (Input 1 on a two-input interface and the only input on an iPhone), so you only need to change it for a second input.
    • 

  • Which side of a stereo output each sound plays on (for example guitar on the left, everything else on the right) is set in the Mixer drawer.
    • 



Recording


    

  • Rec replaces a track and Dub adds a new layer to what is already recorded.
    • 

  • Lead-in plays 0, 4 or 8 clicks before recording starts.
    • 

  • A loop can be up to 5 minutes.
    • 

  • With the drums and scroll ticked, the drums count in, recording begins on the first beat of the first bar, and the loop is rounded to whole bars when you tap Stop so it stays in time with the drums. The tick starts on if the song has a beat; you can change it each time you open the app.
    • 

  • ⇩ Octave on a track shifts it down one octave and keeps it in time. It is handy for a quick bass line played as single notes on guitar into the Bass track (chords sound muddy).
    • 

  • Octave down keeps the original at sets how much of the original stays in the mix, and Undo brings it back.
    • 



Drums and loops together


    

  • Starting the drums with loops saved plays the loops from the first beat.
    • 

  • The drums are the master: Stop or Fade (next to Start scroll in the Play bar) ends the drums and the loops.
    • 

  • The setting When the drums stop, the loops chooses whether they finish their current pass or stop at once.
    • 

  • Pausing or restarting the scroll never affects the drums or loops.
    • 

  • During Play, four rings on the right show Guitar, Voice, Bass and the drum beat. The loop rings count through the loop and turn red while recording; the drum ring flashes with each beat, brighter on strong hits.
    • 



Tempo and timing


    

  • Each loop remembers the tempo it was recorded at.
    • 

  • If you later change the drum tempo, the Loops panel offers Match loops to tempo. It stretches the loops without changing their pitch (best within about 15 percent either way). Undo match restores them until you record again or leave the song.
    • 

  • If the loops sound early or late, change Latency.
    • 



Where loops are kept


    

  • Loops are saved with each song, but only on this device.
    • 

  • They are not part of backup files.
    • 



Foot pedal


    

  • Open Loops, then Foot pedal and keyboard. Tap Learn beside an action, then press the pedal.
    • 

  • Pedals that act like a keyboard (Bluetooth or USB) work. iPad Safari cannot read MIDI directly.
    • 

  • The actions are: Start / stop drums (with loops and scroll), Record or dub (auto), Record / stop, Dub / stop, Play / stop loops, and Next track.
    • 





Sample connection to iPad
An alternative to using the iPad's built-in microphone and speakers: connect a powered USB hub to the iPad, then plug an audio interface and a USB amp or PA into the hub, and your guitar and a microphone into the interface.
More info…
This is one working setup, not the only one. The iPad connects by USB to a hub that has its own power input. The hub carries the audio interface (a Focusrite Scarlett 4th Gen here), which takes the guitar on a 1/4" cable and the microphone on an XLR cable, and the amp or PA (a Spark Live here) as USB audio. The amp's 5 V / 1.5 A charge port powers the hub, so the iPad and the accessories get power through one cable. Then open the Mixer and Loops tabs in Play mode and choose the interface as the input for each of Guitar, Voice and Bass. Your own gear may differ; check that your hub, interface and amp work with the iPad.
Mixer and Pedalboard


The Mixer tab


    

  • Sets the level of each input (Guitar, Voice, Bass) and how loudly you hear it while playing.
    • 

  • Sets the loop and drum volumes and the master volume.
    • 

  • Sets which side of a stereo output each sound uses (LEFT, RIGHT or BOTH SIDES).
    • 

  • A loop's speaker button mutes it; the speaker turns red with a line through it while it is muted.
    • 



The Pedals tab


    

  • Holds a chain of guitar pedals for each of Guitar, Voice and Bass, plus a Pass-through chain for playing solos live through the app. Pass-through has the same Channel choice for its input.
    • 

  • Tap Add pedal, then move it with ▲ ▼, turn it On or off, or remove it with ×.
    • 



Pedals for one song


    

  • Tick Save these pedals with this song to give a song its own pedalboard (all four chains).
    • 

  • Songs without the tick use your normal pedals. Your normal pedals are not changed by what you do in a song that has its own.
    • 

  • Untick it to go back to your normal pedals.
    • 

  • A song's own pedals and drum levels go with the song in backups, copied text, sharing and exports.
    • 



More info…


The pedals


    

  • Overdrive, Fuzz, Delay, Reverb, Chorus, Tremolo, Compressor, Tone EQ, Wah and Octave down.
    • 

  • The chain for a loop is printed into the recording, so it cannot be changed afterwards. To change the sound, record the loop again.
    • 

  • Pass-through is not recorded. Turn off your interface's direct monitoring so you do not hear the guitar twice.
    • 



Getting a chain from Claude


    

  • Tap Copy request for Claude, type the artist, song or sound in the box and tap Copy to clipboard.
    • 

  • Paste it into a chat with Claude (or another assistant), then copy the one line it answers with (it starts SONGTRAK-CHAIN).
    • 

  • Tap Paste a chain…. This is two steps: first the chain replaces the pedals on the chain you are viewing (you are asked first if it already has pedals), so you can play it and change the pedals.
    • 

  • When you like it, edit the name in the name box above the pedals if you want, and tap Save to My chains.
    • 

  • The chain is checked: unknown pedals are left out and values are kept inside each pedal’s range.
    • 

  • It only uses the ten pedals above, so the answer is an approximation of the real sound. You will want to adjust it by ear.
    • 



My chains


    

  • Save to My chains (next to the name box) keeps a chain under My chains…, a list of presets that stays on this device and is shared by all songs.
    • 

  • The starter chains include Hendrix-style fuzz, Blues crunch, Gilmour-style lead and Surf twang.
    • 

  • Use on copies a preset onto the chain you are viewing. A song stores its own copy of the pedals, not a link to the preset, so later changes to or deletion of the preset never change the song.
    • 

  • The first line of the list switches the order between A to Z and newest first (your saved chains, with the date saved, come before the starter chains). The choice is remembered on this device.
    • 

  • To give a chain to another SongTrak user, open My chains…, tap the chain and choose Share. The iPad Share sheet opens (or the chain is copied if sharing is not available), and the other person pastes the line with Paste a chain….
    • 



How the four chains and the tick box fit together


    

  • Guitar, Voice, Bass and Pass-through are four slots.
    • 

  • Without the tick, they are your normal pedals, used by every song that has no pedals of its own.
    • 

  • With the tick, the song gets its own copy of all four chains. What you change while that song is open goes into the song's copy, and your normal pedals wait untouched until you open a song without the tick.
    • 

  • Using a preset from My chains… in a song with its own pedals replaces that song's copy for that slot.
    • 



What is kept where


    

  • Kept on this device only, and not in backup files: your normal pedals, My chains…, the Pass-through input, the Channel choices and the Mixer levels.
    • 

  • A song's own pedals and drum levels are part of the song, so they are in backups.
    • 

  • Loops keep the pedals as they were when recorded.
    • 

  • If a device delivers only one channel, the app uses it as it is and says so (iPadOS sometimes does this).
    • 





8. Share


    

  • Share opens the iPad Share sheet, where you can email, message, WhatsApp, print or tap Save to Files. On a computer, Share ▾ shows a menu instead.
    • 

  • The song is always sent with its key, capo and details, so another SongTrak user gets everything.
    • 

  • Setlists share the same way, with every song included in one file.
    • 

  • If an imported song has the same name as one you already have, you can keep both.
    • 



More info…
    

  • The file is plain text in SongTrak’s ChordPro-style format.
    • 

  • It carries the title, artist, key, capo, style, mood, keywords, tuning, scroll speed, beat and chords, and the names of the setlists it is in.
    • 

  • A setlist file holds every song in the setlist.
    • 

  • The Share sheet belongs to the iPad, so what it offers (Mail, Messages, AirDrop, Files and so on) depends on the apps you have installed.
    • 



9. Import


    

  • Import accepts text from the clipboard, one or more files, or a whole folder.
    • 

  • It reads ChordPro (.pro, .cho, .crd, .chopro), .onsong and .txt files, SongTrak files, and OnSong backup files (.backup).
    • 

  • A zip of text files imports as one song per file.
    • 

  • A text file with {new_song} lines between songs is split into songs.
    • 

  • When you import several songs, the app asks you to confirm first.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Importing adds songs. If a song has the same name as one you already have, SongTrak asks what to do: keep both, update yours with the version in the file, or keep yours and skip the new one.
    • 

  • For a file with many songs you are asked once for the whole file (or choose “Ask me for each song”), not once per song.
    • 

  • A SongTrak backup file is different: Open backup file replaces everything and asks you to confirm first.
    • 

  • If your device will not let you pick a file, copy the text and use From clipboard, or drag the file onto the page in Split View.
    • 



Full screen on iPad


    

  • The ⛶ button at the top switches to full screen when your browser allows it.
    • 

  • For the best result, open SongTrak in Safari, tap Share, choose Add to Home Screen, and launch it from that icon. It then runs full screen with no browser bars.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Added to the Home Screen, SongTrak runs as its own app and works without internet once it has loaded.
    • 

  • Each time you open it with internet it looks for a newer copy, and otherwise it uses the saved one.
    • 

  • The version number beside the name shows which copy you have.
    • 



Backing up your songs


Saving a backup


    

  • Your songs are kept inside the app on this device.
    • 

  • Tap Backup and restore, then Save backup file, now and then to keep a safe copy.
    • 

  • On an iPad or iPhone choose a folder such as iCloud Drive when the list appears.
    • 

  • A yellow bar at the top reminds you when you have changes that are not backed up yet.
    • 

  • If your device greys out the backup file, tap Backup file greyed out? and choose any file.
    • 



Moving songs to another device


    

  • Open the backup file there with Open backup file.
    • 

  • Or use Copy songs as text: it copies them to this device's clipboard. Paste them into a message, email or note to yourself, open that on the other device and copy the text, then use Paste songs from text there.
    • 

  • Apple devices on the same Apple ID that are close together, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on, can paste directly (Universal Clipboard), with no message needed.
    • 



Sample songs


    

  • Add sample songs replaces your songs with sample songs for trying the app (you are asked to confirm first), so use it only on a trial copy.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Your songs are stored in SongTrak’s own storage on this device, not in iCloud and not on a server.
    • 

  • Clearing the browser’s website data, or removing the app, can erase them, so a backup file is your real safety net.
    • 

  • A backup file is plain text holding your songs, setlists, saved patterns and fills. Recorded loops are kept separately on this device and are not in it.
    • 

  • SongTrak also keeps up to three automatic copies inside the app before risky actions such as a restore or a delete. You can find them under Recover songs, then Backups…. They live in the same storage, so they do not protect you if that storage is cleared.
    • 



10. Settings and sync


Settings (the ⚙ button)


    

  • Sets text size, colours, sharps or flats, and light or dark appearance. These apply to the whole app on this device.
    • 

  • App text size (Normal, Large, Extra large, Huge) makes the buttons, labels and menus bigger everywhere, including the beat editor. The song lyrics and chords keep their own size settings.
    • 

  • Every drop-down list shows a small arrow. Tapping it opens a list of big buttons with the current choice ticked.
    • 



Sync between devices


    

  • In Backup and restore, Automatic backup to a folder keeps a backup file up to date in a folder that your cloud service (iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive) already shares.
    • 

  • On each device, choose the same folder.
    • 

  • If two devices change the same thing, the latest change wins.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Text size, the four lyric and chord colours (light and dark, set separately) and the other settings are kept on each device and are not part of your library.
    • 

  • SongTrak has no account or server. The cloud service you choose copies the backup file between your devices.
    • 

  • If two devices change the same song, the later change wins, so tap Back up now before big edits.
    • 



11. Delete, select and undo


Deleting one song


    

  • In the Songs list, swipe a song left to show a red Delete button, tap it and confirm.
    • 

  • A black Undo bar appears at the bottom of the screen for 30 seconds.
    • 

  • Deleted songs are also kept: open Backup and restore, then Recover songs, then Backups… to restore one.
    • 



Removing a song from a setlist


    

  • In an open setlist, swiping a song left shows a red Remove button instead.
    • 

  • It takes the song out of that setlist only; the song stays in your library.
    • 

  • There is no confirmation, but the black Undo bar puts it back in the same place.
    • 



Select several songs


    

  • Tap Select (on its own line below the filters) to tick several songs, and Exit select to leave Select.
    • 

  • While Select is on, the screen shows only the search box, your songs and a bar at the bottom (count, All shown, None, Export, Delete and Exit select). The count shows how many of the songs on screen are ticked.
    • 

  • Delete them together, or Export them as a zip or as one text file, to share by email, AirDrop or Files.
    • 

  • The Export box has an Include song data tick box. Ticked, each song keeps its key, capo, beat, tags and setlists for other SongTrak users. Unticked gives plain ChordPro text for other apps. SongTrak remembers your choice.
    • 



Start again from nothing


    

  • Open Backup and restore, then Reset….
    • 

  • Tick what to clear (or tap Select all), type RESET and tap Reset now.
    • 

  • A backup file of your songs, setlists and drum patterns is saved first. If you cancel that save, nothing is cleared.
    • 

  • Pedalboards, mixer, loops and settings are not in the backup file, so they cannot be brought back.
    • 

  • When it finishes the app restarts. After clearing the songs it stays empty, ready for an import.
    • 



More info…
    

  • Removing a song from a setlist with the swipe does not make a backup and is not kept under Recover songs. Use the Undo bar within 30 seconds, or add the song to the setlist again.
    • 

  • Deleting from the Songs list makes a backup of your library first, then removes the songs.
    • 

  • Undo works for 30 seconds from the black bar.
    • 

  • The last 50 deleted songs are also kept, with their places in your setlists, under Recover songs. Restoring one puts it back in the same setlist positions.
    • 



12. History and Recover songs


History


    

  • The three buttons at the top left are back (‹), history (pocket watch) and forward (›).
    • 

  • Back and forward move one step through where you have been.
    • 

  • The pocket watch shows the list of the songs and setlists you have viewed, and the setlists you opened in the manager (shown as “Manage setlist …”). Tap one to go back to it.
    • 



Recover songs


    

  • Recover songs (in Backup and restore) keeps a log of repairs, up to three automatic backups of your library, and any damaged copy that could not be read, so nothing is lost silently.
    • 



More info…
    

  • History lists up to your last 100 songs, setlists and setlist manager visits, on this device only. It is not in backups.
    • 

  • Recover songs keeps a log of repairs (the latest 100), up to three automatic backups, and any damaged copy that could not be read.
    • 

  • Damaged copies are never deleted automatically.
    • 



Tips


    

  • Edit a song to fix chords at any time.
    • 

  • Save your library as a file now and then as a backup.
    •
